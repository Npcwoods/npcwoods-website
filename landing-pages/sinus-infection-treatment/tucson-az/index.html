Which sinus lane

Day, direction, and red flags matter more than mucus color.

This is not a generic sinus essay. It is the same pattern Chris uses on the national sinus page.

Day 1 to 4

Watch and support it.

Early congestion is often viral or allergy-related. Saline, fluids, rest, and humidified air usually come first. Green or yellow drainage is not proof you need antibiotics.

Day 5 to 7+

Text if you are not turning the corner.

This is the practical pay-attention window, especially with facial pressure, thick drainage, a fever pattern, or symptoms that feel different than your usual Tucson dust allergies.

Better, then worse

That rebound story matters.

If you started to improve, then pressure, headache, fever, or drainage came back harder, include that in the first text. Double worsening is one of the most useful details.

Red flags

Use the ER or in-person care.

One-sided face swelling, high fever, vision change, stiff neck, shortness of breath, or symptoms after a head injury should not wait on a text visit. Banner and other go-in doors are for that.

Do I automatically need antibiotics for a sinus infection? +

No. Many sinus symptoms start viral or allergy-related. Antibiotics make more sense when you are not improving around day 5 to 7, last more than 10 days, become severe, or improve and then worsen again. Drainage color alone does not decide it. Chris reviews your specific pattern.
