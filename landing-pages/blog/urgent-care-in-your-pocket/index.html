Why a text?

A text fits into your day. You can send it from the car line, the break room, or the couch.

You don’t have to sit in a waiting room feeling awful next to other people who feel awful.

You don’t have to get dressed or drive. No app to download. No password to forget.

Just type what’s wrong in your own words. Spelling doesn’t count.

Things people text me about

Burning when you pee. A stuffy, achy face that won’t clear up. A sore throat. Pink, crusty eyes.

Poison ivy from pulling weeds. A rash you can’t figure out. A cold sore that’s starting to tingle.

Not sure if yours fits? Text me anyway. If it’s not a good fit for text, I’ll tell you, and you don’t pay.

A lot of what sends people to urgent care is common stuff. It still feels awful when it’s you. Most of the time, good questions and a clear photo tell me a lot.

If it’s Sunday night and everything else is closed

Your kid’s ear hurts. Or a tooth won’t quit. You do not want a two-hour wait.

Text me. If it’s safe to handle this way, I’ll send you a plan. If you need something from the pharmacy, I send it to the one you pick.

If it’s not safe to handle by text, I’ll tell you straight and point you to the right place.

If you hate the waiting room

Drive. Park. Sign in. Sit under those bright lights. Wait some more.

Or send a text. $59. I read it. You get a written plan you can look back at later.

If you already know me

Same visit as always. I still read every one myself.

How a text visit works

  1. Text (480) 639-4722. Tell me what’s going on, in your own words.
  2. I read it and ask questions. I ask what I need to know. Sometimes I’ll ask for a photo.
  3. I decide if text is safe for this. If it isn’t, I tell you where to go, and you don’t pay.
  4. You get a written plan. If you need something from the pharmacy, I send it to the one you choose.
  5. You pay $59. No membership. No app. No video call.

What to put in your first text

  • Your name and the state you’re in right now.
  • What’s bothering you, and when it started.
  • Anything you’ve already tried.
  • Any health problems you have, and anything you take every day.
  • Any allergies.
  • The name of your pharmacy and the town it’s in.

Don’t stress about getting it perfect. If I need more, I’ll ask. Here’s what a first text can look like:

Hi Chris, I’d like to start a $59 visit. My ear started hurting last night. I’m in Georgia.

Sorry, that’s no fun. I’ve got a few quick questions. Any fever? Is anything draining from the ear?

No fever. A little drainage this morning.

Okay, thanks. Which pharmacy do you use, and what town is it in?

What this is not

This is not an ER. Please don’t text me for any of these. Call 911 or go to the nearest ER instead:

  • Chest pain or pressure
  • Trouble breathing
  • A baby or small child who looks very sick to you
  • Sudden weakness, a drooping face, or trouble talking
  • Bleeding that won’t stop
  • Anything that needs hands on you tonight

I’ll tell you if you need to go in. If I can’t safely treat you by text, you don’t pay. If it’s an emergency, call 911.

What it costs

$59. That’s it. One flat price for the visit.

If you need something from the pharmacy, you pay the pharmacy for that part.

No membership. No monthly fee. And if I can’t treat you by text, you don’t pay me.

Why I read every text myself

Some places hand you off to whoever is free. I don’t do that.

When you text this number, you get me. I’d rather hear your story from you than read about it in a chart later.

And if something isn’t right for a text visit, I’ll say so. That part matters most to me.
