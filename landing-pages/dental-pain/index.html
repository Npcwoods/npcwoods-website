What a Text Visit Can Help With

This visit is for tooth and gum infections that are safe to start by text, as a bridge to your dentist.

Dental abscess

A pus pocket at the root of a tooth or in the gum. Throbbing pain, swelling, a bad taste.

Tooth-root (periapical) abscess

Infection at the tip of the root, often after deep decay or an old root canal.

Gum infection

Swollen, bleeding, sore gums, bad breath, or a tooth that feels loose.

After a tooth pull

New pain, swelling, or a bad taste days after an extraction, including dry socket worries.

Wisdom tooth flare

Swollen, sore gum around a wisdom tooth, often with jaw stiffness.

What Should You Do? A Quick Triage Guide

Mild

Minor swelling, manageable pain

  • Small swelling in the gum or jaw you can touch
  • Pain when chewing on that side
  • No fever, or under 99°F
  • No face swelling beyond the spot

Text today. Don’t wait days; tooth infections get worse with time.

Moderate

Real swelling, throbbing pain

  • Noticeable swelling in the gum, jaw, or cheek
  • Throbbing pain that spreads to the ear or jaw
  • Mild fever (99–101°F) or chills
  • Hard to open your mouth all the way
  • Swollen glands under the jaw

This is the window where a same-day text matters. Text now.

Severe

Emergency. Not a text.

  • Face swelling that is spreading toward the eye or neck
  • Trouble swallowing or breathing
  • Swelling under the tongue or floor of the mouth
  • Fever over 102°F, confusion, or feeling very ill

Call 911 or go to the ER now.

Go to the ER. A text visit is not the right lane for:

  • Tooth trauma or a broken tooth that needs emergency dental surgery
  • An abscess that needs drainage or an extraction right now
  • Severe face swelling that is spreading or affecting your airway
  • Swelling under the tongue or in the floor of the mouth
  • Trouble breathing or swallowing
  • High fever (over 102°F) or signs of a body-wide infection
  • Any sign the infection is spreading to your neck

Text-based telehealth is not for emergencies. Call 911 or go to the ER now.

Important: This is bridge care, not dental care. If treatment is appropriate, it can calm things down. You still need a dentist for the real fix: a filling, root canal, or extraction. Think of it as a pause button so you can get on a dentist’s schedule.

Common Questions

Can a text visit replace the dentist?

No. A text visit is bridge care. When it is safe, Chris can help calm things down while you get on a dentist’s schedule. The tooth still needs a dentist for a filling, root canal, or extraction.

How much does it cost?

$59 flat. No membership, no surprise fees, no paperwork. Follow-up texts about the same problem are included.

How fast will I hear back?

Chris reads every text himself and replies same day, often within hours. If treatment is appropriate, it is sent to your local pharmacy the same day. Pharmacy times vary.

Do I pay before Chris reviews my story?

No. You only pay the $59 if Chris can treat you by text. If your story needs in-person or emergency care, he tells you where to go and you are not charged.

Can I text at night or on a weekend?

Yes. Text any time. Late-night texts are read first thing in the morning, or sooner when Chris is up.

What if it gets worse?

Text Chris. Follow-up is free. He may change the plan or send you to an urgent dental visit or the ER. Trouble breathing or swallowing, swelling under the tongue, or a high fever: call 911.

Can I get ongoing refills?

No. This is short bridge care only. Repeating it without dental work is the wrong plan. You still need to see a dentist.

What states do you serve?

Arizona, Colorado, Georgia, Idaho, Iowa, Montana, Nevada, New Mexico, North Carolina, Oregon, Utah, and Washington, plus Florida by telehealth registration (TPAN3355). You must be in one of these states during the visit.
