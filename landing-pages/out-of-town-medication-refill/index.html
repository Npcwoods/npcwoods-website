Try this first

Call your home pharmacy before you text me.

This step is free, and it is often the fastest fix. Many pharmacies can move your prescription to a store near where you are staying. Call them, tell them where you are, and ask. If it works, you may not need me at all.

If your pharmacy can’t help, or your prescription is out of refills, text me. That is what I am here for.

What to text me

Send me these six things.

  • Where you are right now: city and state.
  • The name and dose on the label. Type it out the way it is printed.
  • How long you have taken it, and who usually prescribes it.
  • When you ran out, or when you will run out.
  • When you will be home.
  • A pharmacy near you, with its street or zip code.

That is it. I read it myself and I will ask if I need more.

What I can and can’t do

A bridge to get you home. Not a new plan.

What I can do
  • Read your story and check that a bridge is safe
  • Send a short-term refill to a pharmacy near you, when it fits
  • Tell you plainly if it does not fit, before you pay
  • Help if you are sick on the trip, too
What I can’t do
  • Refill controlled substances
  • Give long-term refills or take over your care
  • Change your dose or start something new as a “refill”
  • Treat you if you are not in one of my 14 states

The plain rules on refills. A refill is my call, made one person at a time. It is short-term only, just enough to get you home or back to the person who usually prescribes it. I can’t refill controlled substances. Nothing is promised, and you only pay the $59 if I can treat you.

The honest line

When a text visit is the wrong fit

You have missed doses and now feel very sick. You need a controlled substance. You need a long-term plan or a dose change. You are not in one of my 14 states right now. In those cases I will say so, and you will not be charged for a visit I can’t do.

Not for emergencies

Chest pain, trouble breathing, a face or throat that is swelling, signs of a stroke, or very bad bleeding: call 911 or go to the nearest ER. A text visit is not emergency care.

Questions people ask

Straight answers

Maybe. Text me at (480) 639-4722. If it is safe, I can send a short-term refill to a pharmacy near you. It is my call as your nurse practitioner, and it is not promised. You only pay the $59 if I can treat you.

Do I have to be in a certain state?

Yes. You must be in Arizona, Colorado, Connecticut, Florida, Georgia, Idaho, Iowa, Montana, Nevada, New Mexico, North Carolina, Oregon, Utah, or Washington during the text visit. It is about where you are standing, not where you live. That is 14 states, including Florida by telehealth registration.

Can you refill controlled substances?

No. I can’t refill controlled substances by text, ever. Your pharmacy or the person who usually prescribes them is the right call.

How long of a refill will I get?

Short-term only. It is just enough to get you home or back to the person who usually prescribes it. I don’t give long-term refills.

What should I do first?

Call your home pharmacy. Many pharmacies can move your prescription to a store near you. If that does not work, text me.

The text visit is $59 flat, and you only pay if I can treat you. The pharmacy charges for the meds on its own.

Do I need video or an app?

No. It is all by text. No video, no app, no waiting room.
