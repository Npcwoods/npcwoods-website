What it is

What causes the rash?

Poison ivy, poison oak, and poison sumac all have the same oil. It’s called urushiol (you-ROO-shee-all). When it touches your skin, most people get a red, itchy rash. It often makes blisters too.

The oil soaks into your skin fast. That’s why washing right away helps so much. The rash itself may not show up for a while. If you’ve had it before, it can show up in a few hours to a few days. The first time you ever get it, it can take up to 2 or 3 weeks. So it can be hard to know where you got it.

Heads up: The oil sticks to things. Gloves, shoes, tools, golf clubs, dog leashes, and pet fur can all carry it. Touch them later and you can get a brand new rash. Wash them too.
Know the plants

How to spot them.

You don’t have to be a plant expert. These simple tips help.

1
Poison ivy “Leaves of three, let it be.” It grows as a vine or a small bush. The leaves can be shiny or dull. They often turn red in the fall.
2
Poison oak It also has leaves in groups of three. The edges are bumpy, a bit like an oak leaf. It grows as a bush or a vine.
3
Poison sumac A shrub or small tree that likes wet, swampy ground. Each stem has rows of leaves in pairs, with one leaf at the tip.
4
Dead plants count too Dry vines and roots still have the oil. Never burn them. The smoke can carry the oil into your nose, throat, and lungs.
Myths

Four things people get wrong.

I hear these a lot. Here’s the truth.

✗
“Scratching spreads it.” No. The fluid in the blisters does not spread the rash. New spots pop up because some skin got less oil and reacts later. Still, try not to scratch. It can let germs in.
✗
“You can catch it from me.” No. You can’t catch a poison ivy rash from another person’s rash. You only get it from the oil, on the plant or on something it touched.
✗
“Dead plants are safe.” No. The oil stays on dead leaves, vines, and roots for a long time. Wear gloves when you clear brush, even in winter.
✗
“I never get it, so I’m safe.” Not always. Your body can change. Some people get the rash for the first time as adults.
What to expect

How the rash usually goes.

Every rash is a little different. This is a common pattern. It can look like it’s spreading. Most of the time, it’s just new spots showing up where there was less oil.

1
The itch startsYour skin itches where the oil touched. Then red patches, lines, or bumps show up.
2
Blisters may formSmall or big blisters can fill with clear fluid and leak. The itch can be bad enough to wake you up at night.
3
It crusts overThe blisters dry up and crust. The itch slowly calms down.
4
It fadesFor many people the rash clears on its own in 1 to 3 weeks. The first time you get it, it can last longer.
Home care

What you can do at home.

A small, mild rash can often be handled at home. Only do this if you’re sure it’s from one of these plants.

Try these first

✓Wash your skin gently with soap and water as soon as you can. Rinse well with cool water. Clean under your nails.
✓Put on gloves. Wash your clothes, shoes, tools, and your pet.
✓Lay a cool, wet washcloth on the itchy spots.
✓Take short, cool showers, or a short bath with oatmeal or baking soda in it.
✓Leave blisters alone. If one opens, keep the skin on top. It protects the spot underneath.

Prevention

How to avoid the next one.

A few habits go a long way, in the yard or on the trail.

1
Learn the plants Show your kids too. “Leaves of three, let it be” is easy to remember.
2
Cover up Wear long sleeves, long pants, boots, and gloves when you work in brush.
3
Wash fast Wash gently with soap and water right after you touch a plant. Rinse well. Fast washing may make the rash smaller, or even stop it.
4
Wash your gear Clothes, shoes, gloves, tools, and leashes. Wear gloves while you do it. Bathe your dog if it ran through the brush.

Is poison ivy contagious?

No. You can’t get it from another person’s rash or from blister fluid. You only get it from the plant oil. That oil can be on clothes, tools, or a pet, so wash those.
