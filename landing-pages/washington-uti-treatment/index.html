Who a text visit is for

Care for kids, teens, and adults. A parent or guardian runs a visit for a kid. A text visit fits classic, uncomplicated bladder symptoms with no red flags:

  • Burning when you pee
  • Urgency (gotta go NOW)
  • Frequency (every 20 minutes)
  • Lower belly pressure
  • Cloudy or strong-smelling urine
  • Mild discomfort, no fever

Who should NOT use a text visit for a UTI

“I’m a nurse practitioner, not a salesman. If something more serious is going on, I want you seen in person.”

UTIs that keep coming back: Chris can review your pattern, talk through prevention, and tell you whether you need a referral for more workup. If it needs testing in person, he’ll say so plainly.

“If I tell you to go in, I’ll say why in plain words.”

Use urgent care or an ER instead for:
  • Fever over 101°F with pain in your back or side. That’s kidney territory.
  • Fever, chills, or feeling very ill, or repeated vomiting / can’t keep fluids down
  • Pregnancy, or you think you might be pregnant
  • Male UTI symptoms. These need a deeper look to rule out other causes.
  • A child under 2 with urinary symptoms
  • Catheters, stents, or a recent urologic procedure
  • Significant blood in the urine (not just a little pink)
  • Known kidney stones with severe pain, a weakened immune system, or symptoms that get worse on treatment

Not for emergencies. If you have chest pain or trouble breathing, call 911. Who needs in-person care
