You know the loop. Now the shield — without pretending cranberry is a superhero cape.

The short version

  • Pee after sex and do not hold urine forever.
  • Gentle hygiene beats harsh "cleansing."
  • Cranberry evidence is mixed; it is not a treatment.
  • Frequent recurrences may need tailored medical plans.
Prevention without folklore

Some habits help. Some are juice myths.

You cannot wrap your urethra in bubble wrap. You can lower the odds.[1][2]

I care more about boring habits that make sense than about miracle bottles at checkout.

Habits that make sense

Flush, do not incubate.

Pee after sex to flush germs that got nudged forward. Do not hold pee for trophy lengths of time. Wipe front to back. Stay decently hydrated.[2]

Skip harsh soaps and douches on the urethra neighborhood. Irritation is not cleanliness.

Why it mattersThese are low-cost moves. They are not a promise you will never get another UTI.

Cranberry, honestly

A maybe for prevention. Not a treatment.

Cranberry products have mixed proof for prevention in some groups. They are not a treatment once you already have a bacterial UTI.[1][3]

If you like a standardized product and it does not upset your stomach or mess with your warfarin, fine. Do not chug sugar soda "cranberry cocktails" and call it medicine.

When prevention gets medical

Recurrent patterns may need more than tips.

For frequent loops, we sometimes talk post-sex antibiotics, vaginal estrogen in menopause, or other tailored plans — usually after a fuller look.[1]

That is specialized territory. It is not the default first text visit for everyone.

Do

Pee after sex, hydrate, gentle hygiene.

Don't

Treat an active UTI with juice alone.

Quick questions

You asked. I answered.

Does drinking gallons of water fix a UTI?

Fluids help comfort and flushing. They do not replace antibiotics for true bacterial cystitis.

Are probiotics proven?

Evidence is mixed and product-specific. I do not sell miracle capsules here.

Should I avoid sex forever?

No. Timing, peeing afterward, and lubricants that do not irritate are more realistic talks.

Do vitamins prevent UTIs?

No vitamin is a proven UTI vaccine. Be wary of big claims.
