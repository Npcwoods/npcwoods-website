Home / After Your Visit / Pink Eye

Hey, I'm Chris. Pink eye is one of the most common things people text me about.

Good news: most pink eye is not serious. Some kinds don't even need drops.

Here's how to tell what you have, and what actually helps.

The short version

  • Viral is the most common. Watery eye. It clears on its own in 7 to 14 days, like a cold.
  • Bacterial means thick yellow or green gunk. This is the kind antibiotic drops help.
  • Allergic is itchy and watery in both eyes. It does not spread.
  • Pain, vision changes, or a lot of light pain? That needs in-person eye care. Scroll to the red flags.

What is Pink Eye?

Conjunctivitis, better known as pink eye, is swelling of the clear, thin layer (the conjunctiva) that covers the white of your eye and lines your eyelids.

Think of that layer like a clear screen protector. When germs or pollen get on it, your body sends extra blood there to fight back. More blood means a pink, puffy, gunky eye. That red look is your immune system at work.

Not all pink eye needs prescription drops. Viral pink eye clears on its own. Bacterial pink eye, the kind with thick yellow or green discharge, is where antibiotic drops come in.

Symptoms: What to Look For

Pink eye shows up in a pretty steady way. Here's what to watch for:

👁️

Redness

One or both eyes. Sometimes the eyelids too.

🔥

Itching and burning

You want to rub it. Don't. Rubbing spreads it.

✨

Gritty feeling

Like sand is in your eye, even when nothing is there.

💧

Discharge

Watery and clear points to viral. Thick yellow or green points to bacterial.

🌅

Morning crusting

Lids stuck shut when you wake up. Rinse gently with warm water.

💡

Light sensitivity

Mild light bother is common. Strong light pain is a red flag.

How do I know which kind I have?

Look at the gunk. Watery is usually viral. Thick and colored is usually bacterial. Itchy in both eyes with allergies? Probably allergic.

Treating Pink Eye

If your pink eye is viral, I may not prescribe anything. It clears up on its own in 7 to 14 days. Antibiotics only work on bacteria, so they don't help a virus.

If it looks bacterial, the usual plan is antibiotic eye drops or an eye ointment for about 5 to 7 days. Antibiotics work like a lock and key: they fit the bacteria, not the virus. That's why I ask good questions first.

Most antibiotic eye drops are low cost and work fast. Many people feel better within 24 hours. Finish the full course even when your eye looks better.

Red flags: get seen in person

  • Severe eye pain that doesn't get better
  • Big vision changes or blurry vision that doesn't clear with blinking
  • Strong pain from light, beyond mild discomfort
  • Getting worse after 48 hours on antibiotic drops
  • Swelling around the eye, not just redness
  • Only one eye with severe pain (could be something else)

If you have any of these, text me right away. You may need to be seen in person or by an eye specialist.

Drops fight the germs. Home care is what makes you feel better while they work. 👇

Home Care: What Works

Warm vs cold compresses

Bacterial: warm compresses loosen gunk. Allergic: cool compresses calm the itch. 5 to 10 minutes, 2 to 3 times a day.

Gentle cleaning

Use a damp, clean cloth. Wipe from the outer corner toward your nose. Fresh cloth for each eye.

Don't touch or rub

I know it's hard. Touching spreads it and makes swelling worse.

Wash hands a lot

Soap and warm water for 20 seconds, after touching your face and after drops.

Don't share items

Your own towels, pillowcases, eye makeup, and contact cases. Germs live on them.

Take out contacts

Switch to glasses now. Contacts trap germs against your eye. Go back once symptoms are fully gone.

Toss eye makeup

Mascara, liner, shadow used before it started. Germs live in makeup. Buy new once you heal.

How Contagious is Pink Eye?

Viral and bacterial pink eye spread very easily. Allergic pink eye does not spread at all.

⏱️

How long

Viral: 7 to 14 days. Bacterial: about 24 to 48 hours after starting antibiotic drops.

🏫

School and work

Bacterial: back after 24 hours on drops. Viral: stay home until much better, often 3 to 5 days. Check your school's policy.

🚫

Stop the spread

Don't share towels, pillows, makeup, contacts, or glasses. Wash hands. Cover coughs and sneezes.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long is pink eye contagious?

Viral and bacterial pink eye are contagious for about 7 to 14 days. With antibiotic drops for bacterial pink eye, you're usually safe to return to school or work after 24 hours of treatment if symptoms are getting better. Allergic pink eye is not contagious.

Can I wear contacts with pink eye?

No. Take your contacts out and wear glasses while you have pink eye. Contacts trap bacteria and viruses against your eye and make it last longer. Go back to contacts once symptoms are fully gone.

Do I need to throw away my mascara?

Yes. Throw away any eye makeup you used before the infection started. Bacteria and viruses can live on mascara, eyeliner, and eyeshadow. Buy new products once you're better.

Is pink eye always contagious?

No. Viral and bacterial pink eye are very contagious. Allergic pink eye is not contagious at all. It's your immune system reacting to things like pollen, dust, or pet dander.

When can my child go back to school?

For bacterial pink eye: after 24 hours on antibiotic drops. For viral pink eye: many schools want symptoms to be much better, usually 3 to 5 days. For allergic pink eye: as soon as symptoms are under control. Always check your school's policy.
