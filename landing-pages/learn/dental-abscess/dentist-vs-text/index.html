Dentist vs text visit: who fits

I like being useful. I like being honest more. A lot of abscesses need a dentist or the ER. A smaller group fits a text visit as a bridge. Here is the split.

Dentist fixes the tooth A text is the bridge
You
I cannot get a dentist until Thursday. Can I text you today?
Chris
If you are stable, swallowing, and the swelling is local, we can talk. If you are an emergency, Thursday is not the plan. The ER is.

Who often fits a text visit

You are an adult. You are physically in one of the 13 states I serve, including Florida by telehealth registration. You can swallow. You can breathe. The swelling sits over one tooth or one gum spot. You can tell me the story in texts. You understand a consult does not guarantee a prescription.

You also understand the dentist is still on the calendar. I am the bridge, not the repair shop.

Who needs a dentist, not me

You need a tooth opened, drained, or pulled. You have a loose tooth and pus at the gum line that needs cleaning. You need an X-ray. You need a hands-on exam. That is a dentist. I will tell you that even if you hoped I would skip it.

If you already have a dentist who can see you today, go. Do not wait on a text if a chair is open.

Who needs the ER

You met the red flag list. Airway. Swallowing. Rising floor of the mouth. Spreading neck. Closing eye. You look sick in a way that is more than a toothache. Pregnant people with a spreading infection, people with very hard immune systems, and people who cannot keep fluids down often need a building, not a thread.

Kids under 2 are not a text-visit group at all in this practice.
