Clinical overview

What an ingrown toenail is, in plain language

An ingrown toenail happens when the edge of the nail grows into the nearby skin. The big toe is the usual troublemaker. Pressure from the nail can cause pain and swelling; once the skin breaks, bacteria can get in and create a localized infection.

1
Pain at the edgeSharp, throbbing, or tender along one side of the nail
2
Redness and swellingSkin around the nail looks puffy or angry
3
Pus or drainageYellow, white, or cloudy drainage suggests infection
4
Walking hurtsShoes or pressure make the toe flare up
The honest line: antibiotics can treat the infection around an ingrown toenail. They do not permanently fix a nail edge that keeps digging into skin. Recurring cases usually need podiatry.

What may help

Treatment options for an infected ingrown toenail

Chris chooses treatment based on your symptoms, allergies, state, and safety risks. These are common pieces of the plan.

Common antibiotic

Cephalexin

Brand: Keflex

Often used for uncomplicated skin and soft tissue infections when it fits the person and allergy history.

Home care

Warm soaks

15-20 minutes

Soaks can reduce tenderness, loosen crusting, and make the nail fold easier to keep clean.

Local care

Ointment and bandage

Keep it clean

A thin layer of over-the-counter antibiotic ointment and a loose bandage can protect irritated skin.

Long-term fix

Podiatry

For recurring nails

A podiatrist can remove the problem nail edge and prevent repeated digging in.

Safety first

When to go in instead

If this looks bigger than a localized toe infection, skip the text visit and get seen in person.

Go to urgent care or the ER for:

  • Fever or chills with toe infection
  • Red streaks moving up the foot or leg
  • Rapidly spreading redness or swelling
  • Diabetes, poor circulation, or immune suppression
  • Severe pain, numbness, black skin, or inability to walk
  • Worsening drainage despite treatment
Home care

What to do today

This is the part that makes the toe calm down while the infection is being handled.

1

Soak it

Warm water, 15-20 minutes, three to four times daily. Pat dry gently afterward.

2

Stop the pressure

Wear open-toe shoes, sandals, or a roomy toe box. Tight shoes keep the nail digging in.

3

Do not dig

Avoid bathroom surgery. Cutting deep corners can make infection and regrowth worse.

  • Keep the area clean and dry between soaks.
  • Use over-the-counter pain relief as directed on the label if you can take it safely.
  • Trim nails straight across once things heal. Do not round the corners short.

FAQ

Common questions

Can an ingrown toenail be treated online?

Sometimes. NPCWoods can help when the problem looks like a localized bacterial infection around the nail. If the nail edge needs removal, that is a podiatry job.

When do I need antibiotics?

Antibiotics may be appropriate when there is pus, warmth, swelling, spreading redness, or worsening tenderness. A sore nail without infection often starts with soaks, shoe changes, and careful nail care.

Will antibiotics stop it from coming back?

No. Antibiotics can treat infection around the nail. They do not change the shape of the nail or remove the edge digging into skin. Recurring cases need podiatry.

How much does it cost?

The NPCWoods visit is $59 flat. No hidden fees. No paperwork. Medication cost at the pharmacy is separate.

Can I exercise with it?

Rest while the toe is swollen, draining, or very painful. Pressure and sweating can keep it irritated. Return gradually once pain and drainage improve.

When is a podiatrist the better move?

Use a podiatrist for recurring ingrown toenails, severe pain, thick tissue growing around the nail, or when the nail edge needs a minor procedure to stop regrowth into the skin.
