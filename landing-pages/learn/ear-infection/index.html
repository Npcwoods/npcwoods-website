The basics

What is an ear infection?

A middle ear infection (otitis media) happens when fluid gets trapped behind your eardrum and germs start growing in it. That trapped fluid pushes on the eardrum, and that pressure is the deep, throbbing ache you feel. It usually follows a cold, allergies, or sinus congestion that swells the small tube (the Eustachian tube) that normally drains your ear.

Kids and adults both get them

They're most common in young kids, but adults get them too, especially right after a cold or a sinus infection.

Not the same as swimmer's ear

Swimmer's ear is an infection of the outer ear canal. This guide covers middle ear infections, the kind behind the eardrum.

Bottom line: Most ear infections settle down with the right care. A bad one left alone can spread, or the pressure can tear the eardrum, so don't tough out pain that keeps getting worse.

What you'll notice

Symptoms to watch for

Ear pain

Sharp, dull, or throbbing. Often worse lying on that side.

Fullness or pressure

Like your ear needs to pop and won't.

Muffled hearing

Sounds seem far away. Usually comes back as the fluid clears.

Drainage

Clear, yellow, or bloody fluid can mean the eardrum opened up.

Fever

Low-grade to high. More common in kids.

Poor sleep

Pain often peaks at night when you lie flat.

Headache

Pain can spread to your temple or the side of your head.

Dizziness

The inner ear helps with balance, so you may feel off.

In little kids: watch for tugging at the ear, extra fussiness, trouble sleeping, not eating, and fever. Babies can't tell you their ear hurts, so behavior changes are your clue.

How I treat it

Not every ear infection needs a prescription

Plenty of adult ear infections are viral and calm down on their own in a few days with good pain care at home. When yours looks bacterial, or the pain, fever, or drainage tells me it needs more, I'll choose a treatment that fits your history and allergies and send it to your pharmacy.

Mild

Often 48 to 72 hours of watchful waiting with home pain care. If it isn't turning around, text me back and we step it up.

Moderate to severe

Bad pain, fever, or drainage usually means treating it right away so it doesn't spread.

Keeps coming back

Three or more a year, or fluid that won't clear, and I'll point you to an ear, nose, and throat specialist.

Red flags: get emergency care

Call 911 or go to the ER if you have any of these. Don't wait on a text.

  • Severe headache with a stiff neck
  • Swelling, redness, or tenderness behind the ear
  • Face drooping or weakness on one side
  • Fever of 103°F or higher that won't come down
  • Dizziness so bad you can't stand
  • Bloody or pus-like drainage with severe symptoms
  • Hearing loss that gets much worse

Swelling and redness behind the ear plus fever can mean mastoiditis (infection in the bone behind the ear). It's rare, but it's an ER visit.

Feel better faster

Home care that actually helps

Warm compress

Warm (not hot) cloth or heating pad on the sore ear for 10 to 15 minutes, a few times a day.

Sleep sore ear up

Keep the painful side off the pillow and prop your head up. It eases the pressure.

Over-the-counter pain care

Follow the label exactly. Not sure what's safe with your other meds? Ask me or your pharmacist.

Hydrate and rest

Fluids thin out congestion, and rest gives your body room to fight it off.

Chew, swallow, yawn

Gentle jaw movement can help the Eustachian tube open and drain.

Keep it dry

Nothing in the ear canal. No cotton swabs, and keep water out in the shower.

Recovery timeline: most people feel a lot better in 2 to 3 days and are back to normal in about a week. Fluid behind the eardrum can take a few weeks to fully clear, but you'll feel fine long before that.
