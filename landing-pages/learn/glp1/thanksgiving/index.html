By Chris Woods, MSN, APRN, FNP-C, AGACNP-BC
Board-certified nurse practitioner · Reviewed November 18, 2026

Thanksgiving is November 26. People keep asking me two things: will I still enjoy dinner, and should I skip or lower my shot so I can eat more? Here's my plain-talk plan. For everyday eating tips, see Eating on a GLP-1.

The short version

  • Yes, you can still enjoy the meal. Go small and slow.
  • Don't skip or lower your shot on your own. Text me first.
  • Put protein on the plate first. Don't starve all day to save room.
  • If you get severe belly pain or can't keep drinks down, get help — even on a holiday. Call 911 for an emergency.
Will I still enjoy it?

Yes. Make the plate work for you.

The day is about the people at the table, not a clean plate. You can still have the foods you love. Take a little of each thing. Sit down. Slow down. Sip water between bites.[1]

Put protein first — turkey, ham, beans, or eggs. Then vegetables. Then the rich sides and sweets. A GLP-1 can make you feel full sooner, so those first bites matter most.

Don't starve all day to "save room." That plan often leads to a huge plate later. A stuffed, slow stomach can feel rough.[1]

Why it mattersA smaller plate can still be a good plate. You are not missing the holiday. You are matching the meal to a slower stomach.

Your shot

Don't change your dose on your own.

I hear this a lot: "Can I skip or lower my shot so I can eat more?" Please don't do that on your own. Text me first.[1]

Your dose is a plan, not a holiday switch. Skipping it can cause trouble if you also use a GLP-1 for blood sugar. Starting back up can make side effects feel worse for a bit. And one missed shot may not even bring your appetite back the way you hope.[1]

Can I skip this week's shot so I can eat more?

Don't change it on your own. Text me first and we'll talk it through.

Why it mattersWork with the medicine you are already on. If you want a change, I can help you think it through. I won't change a dose just so a plate can be bigger.

A simple plan

Small plate. Protein first. Slow down.

Here's the plan I give most people for the meal itself.[1]

Small plate

Take a little of what you love. You can go back if you are still hungry.

Protein first

Turkey, ham, fish, beans, or eggs first. Then sides.

Slow down

Put the fork down between bites. Give your full signal time to show up.

Don't starve all day

Eat a small breakfast or lunch. Saving every bite for dinner often backfires.

Go easy on grease

Fried and very rich food sits longest in a slow stomach.

Go easy on alcohol

You may feel drinks faster. Don't force a toast if you don't want it.

A 2026 Harvard survey, reported in JAMA Psychiatry, found that 44% of drinkers on a GLP-1 said they drank less.[2] That is what people reported. It is not a treatment claim.

Why it mattersThese are comfort tips, not a diet plan or a promise about weight. Nothing has to be fully off the table.

After the meal

What's normal, and when to get help.

A GLP-1 slows the exit from your stomach. Food leaves bit by bit. So a bigger holiday plate can leave you very full. You may feel queasy. You may feel heartburn. That can happen, and it often eases as the food moves on.[1][3]

Some feelings are a warning, not just "I ate too much." Get help — even on a holiday — if you have:

Severe belly pain

Strong, lasting pain in your belly is not a normal full feeling. Text me or get in-person care.[1]

Can't keep drinks down

If you keep throwing up, or water won't stay down, you can get dried out. That needs help the same day.[1]

Call 911 for an emergency

Chest pain, trouble breathing, or you feel like you might pass out? Call 911. Don't wait on a text.

For more on side effects and red flags, see Side Effects, Honestly.

Why it mattersA stuffed, queasy feeling can be common after a big plate. Severe pain or no drinks staying down is not. Get help even if it is a holiday.

Holiday travel

Pack your pen the safe way.

If you fly, keep your pen in your carry-on, not a checked bag. Keep it in the original packaging if you can.[1]

Follow the storage instructions that came with your pen, or the ones on the drug maker's official site. Different pens have different rules. I won't list temperatures here, because the safe range depends on yours. Don't freeze it. If a pen got very warm, froze, or you're not sure it's still safe to use, text me and check the maker's instructions before you take that dose.

Why it mattersA holiday trip should not mean guessing about storage. The label in your hand is the rulebook for your pen.

Quick questions

You asked. I answered.

Will I still enjoy Thanksgiving dinner on a GLP-1?

Yes. Take a little of what you love, put protein first, and slow down. The day is about the people at the table, not a clean plate.

Should I skip or lower my shot so I can eat more?

No, not on your own. Text me first. Skipping a dose can cause trouble, and it may not even bring your appetite back the way you hope.

What if I feel sick after a big meal?

Feeling very full, a little queasy, or some heartburn can happen because food leaves your stomach slowly. Get help if you have severe belly pain or you can't keep drinks down. Call 911 for chest pain, trouble breathing, or if you feel like you might pass out.

Can I drink at dinner?

You can, but go easy. You may feel drinks faster, and alcohol can worsen an already upset stomach. A 2026 Harvard survey, reported in JAMA Psychiatry, found that 44% of drinkers on a GLP-1 said they drank less. Don't force a toast.

How should I travel with my pen?

Keep it in your carry-on, not a checked bag. Follow the storage instructions from the drug maker. Don't freeze it. I don't list temperatures here because they differ by pen.

When should I text you over the holiday?

Text me if you want to change your dose, if you feel worse than "too full," if you're unsure about a pen, or if you want a plan before the meal. A text visit is $59 flat. You only pay if a text visit is appropriate. Call 911 for an emergency.
