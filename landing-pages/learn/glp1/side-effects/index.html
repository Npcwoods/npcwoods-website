GLP-1 Side Effects, Honestly

I'm not going to sugarcoat this. Most people get some tummy effects. Most of those get better. A few problems are rare and serious. You deserve both lists.

Common side effects

FDA labels list common effects. These include nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, and constipation. Belly pain, bloating, reflux, and burping are common too. Some people feel tired. Hair changes can happen. Results vary.

Why the tummy drama? Remember the yellow light. Your stomach empties slower. A huge greasy meal can sit there. Your gut will complain.

Tips that actually help

  • Eat smaller meals. Stop when you feel full — even if the plate isn't empty.
  • Drink water through the day. Dehydration makes everything worse.
  • Go easy on fried and very fatty food, especially early on.
  • Dose step-ups exist for a reason. Climbing slowly gives your gut time to adapt.
Why this matters: A lot of "I can't tolerate this" stories are really "I ate like day zero." We fix the plate before we give up on the medicine.

Go to the ER / call 911 for these

  • Severe belly pain that goes to your back — possible pancreatitis. Do not wait it out.
  • Gallbladder-type pain — strong right-upper pain, maybe after fatty food, maybe with fever or yellowing skin.
  • Dehydration / kidney trouble signs — can't keep fluids down, very dry mouth, little urine, feeling faint.
  • Allergic reaction — swelling of face or throat, trouble breathing, widespread hives.
  • Low blood sugar if you also take insulin or a sulfonylurea — shakiness, sweating, confusion. Treat per your plan and get help if severe.

Boxed warning (thyroid C-cell tumors)

FDA labels for semaglutide and tirzepatide have a boxed warning. Rodents got thyroid C-cell tumors. We do not know if that matches humans. Do not use these if you or your family had medullary thyroid cancer (MTC). Also skip them if you have MEN2.

Tell me about neck lumps. Tell me about trouble swallowing. Tell me about short breath or a hoarse voice that will not quit. Those need a real look.

Not for pregnancy

These medicines are not for pregnancy. Labels say to stop if you become pregnant. Plan ahead. The medicine can stay in your body for weeks. If you might be pregnant, or plan to be, text me first.

When to text me vs when to go to the ER

Text me
Mild nausea. Food questions. Dose timing. Constipation tips. Whether to wait on a step-up. "Is this normal?" check-ins.
ER / 911
Severe pain to the back. Breathing trouble. Face or throat swelling. Collapse. Bad dehydration. Anything that feels like an emergency. Do not text and wait.

FAQ

What side effects are most common?

Nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, constipation, belly discomfort, reflux, burping, and fatigue are among the common ones listed on FDA labels.

How do I make nausea better?

Smaller meals. Stop when full. Sip fluids. Skip greasy, fried food. Dose step-ups exist because jumping high too fast makes tummy effects worse.

When is it an emergency?

Severe belly pain that goes to the back, signs of a bad allergic reaction, or symptoms of severe dehydration need emergency care. Call 911 if you are in danger.

Who should not use these medicines?

People with a personal or family history of medullary thyroid cancer or MEN2 should not use them, per boxed warnings. They are not for pregnancy.
