Okay, science time — but keep your shoes on. I'm going to use doorbells, locks, keys, and a bouncer. If those stick, you understand GLP-1 better than half the internet.

Your gut sends the first text

After you eat, special cells in your intestine called L-cells release GLP-1. That hormone travels and talks to GLP-1 receptors. A receptor is a G-protein coupled receptor — fancy words for "a lock on the cell that only certain keys open."

Where the locks sit

  • Pancreatic beta cells — when sugar is high, they release more insulin (glucose-dependent).
  • Pancreatic alpha cells — they release less glucagon, so the liver dumps less sugar.
  • Stomach — emptying slows. Food hangs out longer. That yellow-light feeling.
  • Brain — hypothalamus and brainstem help with appetite and fullness.
GLP-1 = doorbellTells the brain and body: dinner is here.
Receptor = lockSitting on cells waiting for the right key.
Medicine = keyFits the lock and holds it open longer.
DPP-4 = bouncerKicks natural GLP-1 out in about two minutes.

Why natural GLP-1 is gone in a blink

DPP-4 is an enzyme that chops GLP-1 almost right away. So your own hormone is a short text. The medicines are written to dodge that bouncer. Weekly shots are built to stick around on the order of a week.

Tirzepatide also hits GIP receptors. Extra key. Extra lock. Same neighborhood of gut hormones.

Yellow light for the stomach

Slower gastric emptying means sugar from a meal enters the blood more slowly. It also means that greasy triple burger can sit there and make you regret every choice. That's why nausea shows up when people eat like nothing changed.

FAQ

Where does natural GLP-1 come from?

Intestinal L-cells make GLP-1 after you eat. It is a short-lived gut hormone.

Why do the medicines last so long?

Natural GLP-1 is broken down in about two minutes by DPP-4. The medicines are built to resist that and often last about a week.

What is different about tirzepatide?

Tirzepatide hits both GLP-1 and GIP receptors. Think of it as a key that opens two related locks.

Will this lower my blood sugar too much?

GLP-1 effects on insulin are mostly glucose-dependent. Low sugar risk goes up if you also take insulin or a sulfonylurea. Tell me what you take.
