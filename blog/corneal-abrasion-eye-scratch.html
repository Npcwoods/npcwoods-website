Here’s the deal: the cornea is the clear windshield over the front of your eye. It does a lot of important work for your vision, so even a small scratch can hurt like crazy.

A fingernail, makeup brush, contact lens, dust, or a little grit from yard work can all irritate or scratch that surface. The frustrating part is that a scratch, a stuck particle, an infection, and some deeper injuries can overlap on symptoms. That is why this guide is about smart next steps—not trying to name the problem from a screen.

What a scratched eye can feel like

A corneal abrasion often brings pain, tearing, redness, light sensitivity, blurry vision, or a gritty foreign-body feeling. Blinking can make it feel worse because your lid is moving across an irritated surface. You may remember exactly what happened—or you may only know that something feels very wrong.

Those symptoms are real, but they do not confirm a scratch. A clinician needs to look at the eye, check vision, and sometimes use fluorescein dye to see what is going on.

What to do right now

  • Do not rub your eye. It can make an irritated surface feel worse.
  • Wash your hands before touching around your eye or taking out a contact lens.
  • For a chemical splash, flush with clean running water for 15 minutes, then get emergency care.
  • Do not try to remove an object that is stuck in your eye. Protect it from more pressure and get urgent help.
  • Take out contact lenses and do not put them back in until a clinician says it is safe.

What not to do

A few old-school ideas are not helpful here. Do not patch your eye unless a clinician specifically tells you to. Eye patches do not improve comfort or healing for a routine abrasion and can delay healing. Do not use numbing or medicated eye drops unless a clinician directs you to use them.

And please do not wait on a message if you have any red flag above. When it comes to vision, the safest move is being seen in person when the situation calls for it.

Why contact lenses, chemicals, and high-speed debris change the plan

Contact lenses can raise the concern for infection when the eye surface is injured. Chemicals can keep damaging tissue until they are flushed out. And high-speed debris can cause a deeper injury that you cannot safely judge at home.

None of that means the worst is happening. It means these are the situations where an in-person eye exam is the right call.

What an in-person eye exam can check

An in-person clinician can check how well you see, look for an object tucked under the lid, examine the pupil and front of the eye, and use fluorescein dye to look for a surface defect. Those details matter because the same red, painful eye can have different causes and different levels of urgency.

That is why a text conversation cannot confirm a corneal abrasion. Text is useful for helping you make a safe decision about where to go next—not for skipping an exam you need.

How long does a corneal abrasion take to heal?

Many uncomplicated surface abrasions heal quickly—often in a day or two, sometimes within two to three days. But that only applies after serious injury and infection have been ruled out.

If symptoms are getting worse, your vision is off, the pain is not easing, or you are not seeing improvement after 24 hours, get checked. Do not try to push through an eye problem just because the original scratch seemed small.

How to prevent another eye scratch

Wear proper eye protection when you are using tools, working around chemicals, doing high-speed work, or playing a sport where fingers and flying objects happen fast. Give your contact lenses a break when your eye is irritated, and keep makeup brushes and hands away from your eyes when they are not clean. It is simple stuff, but it protects something you use every second of the day.

Common Questions

Can a scratched eye heal on its own?

Some uncomplicated surface scratches heal quickly. The hard part is knowing whether it is actually a simple surface scratch. Get an in-person eye exam for red flags, worsening symptoms, or no improvement.

Should I go to urgent care for a scratched eye?

Go for same-day in-person eye care if your vision changes, pain is severe, a chemical or high-speed object was involved, something is stuck, or you have blood, pus, or a misshapen pupil. Emergency care is the right move for serious trauma or chemical exposure.

Can I wear my contacts with a scratched eye?

No. Take them out and use glasses until a clinician says it is safe to restart them. Contact lenses can increase the concern for infection when the surface of the eye is injured.

What if something is stuck in my eye?

Do not try to pull it out. Especially after metal work, glass, or high-speed debris, get urgent in-person care so the eye can be checked safely.
