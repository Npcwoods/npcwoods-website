I feel awful and don’t want to sit in urgent care

That is a normal reason people look past big telehealth and the lobby.

You do not have to spend half a day in a waiting room for a problem that often fits a text visit. If you are not in immediate danger, text Chris what hurts in your own words. He reads it himself, asks what he needs, and sends a written plan. When medication is appropriate, it goes to your pharmacy.

Same goal as urgent care or a big telehealth app: get you a clear next step. Different demand on your day — couch, not lobby; text, not video queue.

Go to the ER or call 911 for chest pain, trouble breathing, severe allergic reaction, stroke signs, uncontrolled bleeding, or anything that feels life-threatening. Text care is not emergency care.

What a simple sick visit by text can cover

If it is safe to handle by text, Chris will. Common $59 visits include:

  • UTI symptoms (burning, urgency, frequency — with red-flag screening)
  • Sinus and throat (pressure, congestion, sore throat, fever patterns)
  • Ear pain and lingering cough / bronchitis-style symptoms
  • Pink eye, rashes, and other skin issues that need trained eyes
  • Other straightforward acute problems when a hands-on exam is not required
Not the right fit: controlled substances (no opioids, benzos, or stimulants), problems that need an exam / imaging / labs, chronic disease management better done with a PCP, kids under age 2 for most issues, or anything rapidly worsening or scary.

If Chris cannot safely sort it by text, he will say so — and you do not pay for a visit he cannot do.

Sinus care without the clinic lobby

Many sinus infections improve with self-care first: rest, fluids, saline rinse with sterile water, pain relief as directed on the label, and short use of decongestant sprays. Antibiotics are not automatic.

Text makes sense when symptoms have dragged on (often around 10 days or more), got better then worse, or you need a licensed clinician to screen for danger signs and decide if treatment by text is safe. Seek in-person care for severe headache, vision change, swelling around the eye, stiff neck, high fever, or trouble breathing.

Other alternatives people compare (honest short take)

People shopping “alternatives to Teladoc” also see cash chat options and hospital virtual visits. Pick by the job:

  • Want a named NP, no video, no app, pay only if treatable → NPCWoods ($59 text).
  • Want the lowest sticker on a large multi-state chat network → compare current cash rates on those apps (prices change; check their site).
  • Have work benefits that already cover Teladoc → start there; a covered visit can beat every cash option.
  • Need stitches, imaging, or you look seriously sick → urgent care or ER, not text.

No single alternative wins every case. Match the tool to the problem.
