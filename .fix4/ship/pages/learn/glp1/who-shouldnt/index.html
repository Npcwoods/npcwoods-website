By Chris Woods, MSN, APRN, FNP-C
Board-certified nurse practitioner · Reviewed October 6, 2026

Eating tips help when a GLP-1 fits. First we make sure it is even on the table for you.

The short version

  • Personal or family MTC history, or MEN2, means these medicines are not for you.
  • They are not for pregnancy — we plan timing if you could conceive.
  • Pancreatitis, gallbladder, and severe gastroparesis need a careful look.
  • A consult does not guarantee a prescription.
Clear stops

Some histories mean no.

I am Chris Woods, a nurse practitioner. Part of my job is saying no.

Brand labels list hard stops. One is a personal or family history of medullary thyroid cancer (MTC). Another is MEN2, a rare hormone syndrome.[1][2][3][4]

Why it mattersThese are label rules, not vibes. If this is your history, we pick another path.

Pregnancy and planning

Not for pregnancy.

These medicines are not for pregnancy. If you could get pregnant, we plan timing. We also plan when to stop before you try to conceive. We follow the label for your medicine.[1][2]

Breastfeeding has its own rules. Tell me if you nurse or plan to.

Why it mattersGrowing a baby and changing gut hormones do not mix. We plan ahead.

Extra caution lists

We dig deeper here.

Past pancreatitis needs a careful look. So does serious gallbladder disease. So does a stomach that already empties very slowly (gastroparesis). Eye and kidney history can matter too.[1][2][5]

Other medicines matter. Ones that change blood sugar or stomach speed need a close look.

Pancreatitis history

Tell me about severe belly pain episodes in the past.

Slow stomach already

If food already sits for hours, stacking more slow may not fit.

Blood sugar meds

We watch lows if you also use insulin or similar tools.

Allergies

A past serious reaction to the same medicine class matters.

Why it mattersCaution is not always a hard no. But it is never a shrug.

How I screen

Your story first.

Before we talk about a prescription, I ask about thyroid family history. I ask about pregnancy plans. I ask about pancreas and gallbladder history. I ask about other medicines. I ask what you hope for.

A consult does not guarantee a prescription. If you are not a fit, I will say so. Then we talk about next steps.

Why it mattersSaying no is part of good care. I would rather keep you safe than rush a refill.

Quick questions

You asked. I answered.

I had a thyroid nodule. Is that an automatic no?

Not always. MTC and MEN2 are the hard label stops. Bring your records so we can sort the type.

Can I start if I might want a baby next year?

We plan timing and when to stop before trying. Tell me your timeline early.

What if I had pancreatitis years ago?

It is a major caution. We may choose a different path. Do not hide it.

Does a consult mean I get a prescription?

No. A consult is a fit check. A prescription only happens if it is safe and appropriate.
