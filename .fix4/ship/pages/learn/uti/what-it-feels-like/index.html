By Chris Woods, MSN, APRN, FNP-C
Board-certified nurse practitioner · Reviewed October 6, 2026

Last stop, the germ climbed. Now the bladder complains. Here is what that complaint usually sounds like in real life.

The short version

  • Burn when you pee is called dysuria.
  • Urgency is the sudden "NOW" signal.
  • Frequency means many trips with little pee.
  • Fever or flank pain is not the simple bladder script.
Feeling 1 · Burn

Pee that stings is the classic alarm.

Clinicians call it dysuria. You may say, "It burns when I pee." That burn is the angry bladder and urethra talking.[1][2]

It can feel like a hot scrape at the start of the stream, or all the way through.

Why it mattersBurn alone is not the whole story. Pair it with urgency and frequency, and a bladder UTI climbs the suspect list.

Feeling 2 · Urgency + frequency

Your bladder hits the panic button.

Urgency means the "I need a bathroom RIGHT NOW" wave. Frequency means you just went, and you need to go again.[1]

Often only a little pee comes out. That is the irritated bladder wall, not a giant lake of urine.

Feeling 3 · Pressure

A dull low-belly weight.

Many people feel pressure or mild pain above the pubic bone. It can feel like a small rock sitting there.[2]

Blood in the pee can happen with a bladder UTI. Pink or rusty tint is possible. Big clots or heavy bleeding is a different problem — text me or get seen.

Not the usual bladder script

Fever and flank pain change the story.

A simple bladder UTI (cystitis) usually keeps you out of the ER. Fever, shaking chills, side or back pain under the ribs, vomiting, or feeling wiped out can mean the kidney is involved.[3]

That is the next big fork in the road: bladder vs kidney.

Common

Burn, urgency, frequency, low pressure.

Red flags

Fever, flank pain, vomiting, feeling very sick.

Pee clues

Cloudy, strong smell, or pink tint can show up.

Gray zone

Mild symptoms that are new still deserve a real review.

Cheat sheet

Keep this pocket card.

Dysuria

Burn or sting with pee.

Urgency

Sudden need to go.

Frequency

Many trips, small amounts.

Red flags

Fever, flank pain, vomiting — think kidney.

Quick questions

You asked. I answered.

Can a UTI cause back pain?

Low belly pressure is common. Pain in the side or back under the ribs with fever is more concerning for a kidney infection.

Is cloudy pee enough to diagnose a UTI?

No. Smell and cloudiness can have other causes. Symptoms plus a careful history matter.

Do I always see blood?

No. Some people do, some do not.

How fast do symptoms show up?

Often over hours to a day or two after the climb begins.
